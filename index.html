01

What it asks

one request · five slots · three grades

Literature search is no longer a keyword problem. The frontier moves weekly, and the papers that matter are often a few citations away from the obvious ones. FrontierLitBench measures whether an agent can surface them under a fixed protocol.

Each task names a research track, an allowed set of publication years and a venue whitelist. The system returns exactly five papers, ranked. Scoring uses only pre-frozen identity, track, year, venue and tier labels — never the system's own claims.

Out-of-scope recommendations score zero. Genuinely new eligible papers are adjudicated once, for every system, before any score is computed.

02

How it is built

frozen before any system is scored
i

Candidate pool

Conference catalogues 2024–2026, widened with sub-topic queries so recall is not decided by citation count.

ii

Verified identity

DOI, arXiv and normalised title are cross-checked; preprints are merged with their accepted version.

iii

Impact grade

Q = 0.55·citations + 0.30·GitHub stars + 0.15·institution, percentile-normalised within track and year.

iv

Tiers S / A / B

Top 20% / next 40% / last 40% per track. Thresholds are locked; new papers reuse them rather than moving the bar.

Library v1.1 — 425 original gold papers plus 332 adjudicated additions: 757 eligible (S 127 · A 351 · B 279), 31 rejected. IDCG fixed at 14.7423.

04

Three metrics

linear gains 5 / 3 / 1 / 0 · log₂ position discount

nDCG@5 primary

DCG = Σ g(pᵢ) / log₂(i+1)

Ranking quality. The same paper at rank 2 is worth less than at rank 1 — the discount is what makes ordering count.

WeightedHit@5

Σ g(pᵢ) / 25 × 100

Total quality of the five slots, order-blind. Separates “picked good papers” from “picked them in a good order”.

S-Tier Hit@5

Σ 1[g(pᵢ) = 5]

How many of the five are S-tier. Read it next to the number of S papers available, since difficulty varies by task.
